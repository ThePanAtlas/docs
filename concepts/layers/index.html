    Knowledge layers¶

    panAtlas separates what a thing is from what is claimed about it, and claims that panAtlas has verified from claims that were seeded from a source or derived by computation. The word "layer" is used for several of these separations in the repository. This page reconciles them with the code and states what lives in each layer today.

    Sources: GRAPH_STANDARDS.md (decision record, adopted 2026-07-26, extended 2026-09-26), LAYERS.md, atlas/CLAUDE.md, and the build scripts and data at repo HEAD 57505c6. Counts were computed on 2026-11-10.

    Four uses of "layer"¶

    Where Layers What it separates
    GRAPH_STANDARDS.md (1) terminology backbone, (2) clinical instance layer, (3) working property graph Kinds of content in the one RDF graph
    LAYERS.md substrate, connectivity, embeddings Identity, claims, and the models that produce claims
    The markdown atlas (atlas/CLAUDE.md) human-readable, machine-readable, evaluation, registry, governance pages Page types of the git-backed fact graph
    The layer attribute in graph.json foodatlas, concept, dataset, fndds, ontology, process, domain A display partition for the explorer (Graph schema)

    These are not competing models. The first two describe the same graph from two angles (content type, and identity versus claim). The atlas is where curated, verifiable facts are written. The layer attribute is a user-interface grouping and carries no meaning beyond that.

    The three graph layers of GRAPH_STANDARDS.md¶

    The decision: one RDF graph, three layers, joined by concept IRIs.

    The decision record was written for medicine, the domain the atlas started with, and its wording is medical: layer 1 is "the medical concepts and entities" and layer 2 is the "clinical / multi-modal instance layer". The pattern itself is domain-agnostic. Each domain's concepts come from that field's own vocabularies and join the same graph by canonical identifier; the served graph already does this for foods (FoodOn, FoodData Central) and for general concepts (Wikipedia). Backbones and instance models for other domains are not yet written into the standard.

    Layer What it is What lives in it today Status
    1. Terminology / ontology backbone Concepts and entities with stable identifiers from real vocabularies, SKOS-shaped. For medical concepts MeSH is canonical and the hierarchy comes from MeSH tree numbers 3,390 nodes with a mesh: canonical id (among them 51 curated concepts, 142 MeSH tree expansions and 3,160 FoodAtlas diseases); 3,360 part_of edges from tree numbers; cross-codes; 15 code-system authority nodes; Wikipedia-grounded general concepts. See Controlled vocabularies Built
    2. Clinical / multi-modal instance layer Actual records whose codings carry concept IRIs that join layer 1. For medicine, FHIR RDF R4 (ImagingStudy, Observation, Condition, …) The 13 FHIR resource types and 5 NACC record types as nodes; 36 instantiates edges saying which datasets would hold which resource. No instance records Designed
    3. Working / evidence property graph What the explorer renders: datasets, measured metadata, modality, embeddings, evaluation items and results. A projection of layers 1 and 2, not a separate source of truth The served graph.json (6,187 nodes, 17,119 edges) and its RDF export in the named graph https://panatlas.net/graph/panatlas Built

    The join is the reason this is one graph: a FHIR coding a sct:… or a loinc:… lands on the same concept IRI as the backbone and as the mirrored external graphs. The concept IRIs in use are the rdf_iri_stem values of web/codesystems.json (RDF vocabulary).

    Source of record¶

    Since 2026-09-26 every triple lives in exactly one named graph, and that graph is its source of record. panAtlas curates, verifies and exports only https://panatlas.net/graph/panatlas. External graphs (the NSF OKN graphs, Wikidata) are mirrored read-only under their publisher's graph IRI and replaced wholesale when their agent syncs a new upstream version. panAtlas never edits an externally maintained node or edge; it only links to it (skos:exactMatch, dataset and document annotations) from its own graph. A dataset maintained elsewhere carries maintained_externally: true and a source_of_record block (Dataset registry).

    Substrate, connectivity and embeddings (LAYERS.md)¶

    Substrate: identity¶

    The substrate is the set of canonical grounded entities: what a thing is. A node's identity is its canonical_id (mesh:D051436, fhir:Condition, chebi:15377, foodon:…). Two systems that emit the same canonical_id are talking about the same entity, with no reconciliation. Ungrounded items stay in a private namespace and do not enter the shared substrate until they are grounded. The substrate answers only "does this entity exist, and what is it?"

    In the served graph 5,013 of 6,187 nodes carry a canonical_id; the prefixes in use are mesh (3,390), foodon (1,558), wikipedia (24), fdc (24) and panatlas (17, internal artifact-type vocabulary). The other 1,174 nodes (datasets, benchmarks, models and other non-concept kinds, plus 118 concepts and 10 diseases) have none; see Graph schema.

    Connectivity: claims¶

    Edges are attributed claims about how the substrate relates, not identity. Many overlapping connectivity graphs can sit over one substrate, and a connectivity layer can be swapped or re-derived without touching the substrate. LAYERS.md names four origins:

    Origin Examples in the served graph Marker on the edge
    ontology-tree part_of from MeSH tree numbers (3,360); FHIR binds (15) src: mesh_tree
    curated part_of from curated pages (1,845); prereq (6) src: curated
    embedding-derived classified_as from the corpus classification (1,749 with n_docs); related (31 with sim); benchmark covers_topic from the judge-gated item mapping (300 with n); covers_topic from arXiv lexical alignment (203) n_docs, sim, n, basis
    agent-contributed Edges reported over the federation by site agents claims (in agent reports)

    Every edge is meant to carry an origin and evidence (for example {model, version, cosine}), so that connectivity merges as claims and conflicts surface instead of being overwritten.

    The served graph does not carry origin and evidence

    No edge in graph.json has an origin or evidence attribute. Provenance is partial and uses other names (src on 5,715 edges, via on 437, basis on 216); 10,863 edges carry none of the three. The attach process (below) implements the contract as one nested attribute, claims: {origin, model, version, cosine, canonical_id}, on the edges it reports.

    Embeddings and the attach process¶

    Embeddings are how new items find their place in the connectivity graph. Two rules:

    1. Each modality registers its own models in web/models.json.
    2. An embedding is comparable only within one (modality, model, version) space. Cross-modality relation goes through the substrate (shared canonical_id), never through embedding arithmetic.

    A registered model carries its space definition: dimension, pooling, concept anchors (one vector per grounded concept, aligned by slug and canonical_id), the optional centroid taxonomy, and the unknown cutoff. At HEAD only the text model is active (BioClinical-ModernBERT-large, version v3w-2026.07, 1024-d); the imaging entry (RAD-DINO) holds embeddings but no anchors, and genomics and structured data have no registered embedder (Models and curriculum).

    The attach process (web/embed_attach.py) runs per agent, locally, in one model's space:

    new item + embedding e (in model M's space)
        |  L2-normalise (and whiten with the anchors' mu/sd when present); cosine to M's anchors
        v
   top-k concepts by cosine
        |  >= M's cutoff (the whitened cutoff when whitened)?
        |-- yes -> classified_as edges, claims {origin: embedding, model, version, cosine, canonical_id}
        '-- no  -> UNKNOWN: no edge; the item stays in the agent's private namespace

    Its output is connectivity, never substrate. A site's local agent (web/pa_local_agent.py) holds the graph index and embedding cache, runs attach on local embeddings, and emits a structure-only report: grounded nodes (canonical_id, kind, label), edges, and a sha256 content hash, with no documents. The report travels the federation, where entities merge by canonical_id and connectivity accumulates as attributed claims. See Distributed panAtlas and The agent contract.

    Imaging model named in LAYERS.md

    LAYERS.md says imaging is embedded by the LeJEPA-3D CT encoder. The registry's imaging entry is stock RAD-DINO, with anchors held. LAYERS.md also says the local agent's report is served by a separate plugin; the plugin is outside this repository and was not checked here.

    Verified, seeded and derived facts¶

    panAtlas's first principle (atlas/CLAUDE.md): sources are evidence, not authority. Citing a source records where a claim was seen; it never makes the claim true. Only panAtlas verification, a human verified_by sign-off, makes a page verified. FHIR records and vocabulary assertions enter the same way, as evidence with provenance, and become panAtlas facts only after curator sign-off.

    Three kinds of content therefore sit side by side, and the data marks which is which.

    Seeded: evidence waiting for verification¶

    Content Marker
    Concept pages seeded from Wikipedia status: draft, confidence: estimated, a pinned wikipedia: {title, oldid, retrieved}, and a needs-verification callout: "Wikipedia saying it does not make it true"
    Fact pages drafted by an agent status: draft, and the claim tagged "UNVERIFIED (agent-drafted from model knowledge; NOT yet checked against a source)"
    Datasets taken from the inventory built_by "from the dataset inventory; not yet measured …" (88 nodes); measured: false (116)
    Contamination labels that were guesses Downgraded to UNCHECKED by hygiene_enrich.py (122 dataset nodes), with the guess kept as contamination_prior
    Benchmark decontamination not yet run decontam.holdout_status: unchecked (85 of 106 language benchmarks)

    Derived: computed from other data¶

    Content How it is derived Marker
    Concept hierarchy (medical concepts) MeSH tree-number prefixes part_of with src: mesh_tree
    Disease specialty MeSH tree number of the disease built_by: "…specialty by tree number"
    Corpus → concept Corpus document classification (embedding cells + LLM-judged cell labels) classified_as with n_docs
    Concept relatedness Cosine between concept anchors, kept at 0.72 or above related with sim
    Benchmark topics Item → concept mapping (judge-gated) covers_topic with n; node topics_status
    Benchmark grounding Rule: a benchmark reaches a MeSH-grounded concept through covers_topic, tests or covers grounded_in with via: mesh:<UI>
    Per-concept accuracy Per-item correctness joined to the item mapping measured_on with acc, n; eval_accuracy with ckpt
    Artifact type of a benchmark Rule from the task_format facet unless overridden artifact_type_via
    Composite model score Mean over the fixed 21-benchmark battery overall, battery_n

    A derived value is only as good as its inputs, and the inputs are named on the edge or node so the value can be recomputed.

    Verified: checked, with the check recorded¶

    There are two senses of "verified" in the data, and they must not be confused.

    Curator verification is the verified_by sign-off that makes an atlas page status: verified. At HEAD it has not happened for any page: of the 75 typed pages in the repository's markdown atlas, 74 have status: draft and 1 (a fact) has status: in_review; none is verified. 53 carry confidence: estimated and 1 confidence: measured. No node or edge in the served graph has verified_by.

    Machine verification is a recorded, repeatable check of a specific fact against its source. This is what the served graph's verified content rests on:

    What Check Where recorded
    MeSH groundings of curated concepts UTS resolver, then one adversarial agent per concept confirming the descriptor with a live UTS call built_by on 51 concept nodes
    Artifact-type groundings Live NLM MeSH lookup (match=exact) and Wikipedia REST, 2026-08-08; concept-identity judge, 2026-08-09 pipeline/groundings_verified.json; grounding_match, grounding_judge
    Curriculum facts Every job id, config path and repository must exist in the vault (check_curriculum_vault.py, 61/61 on 2026-10-14) Curriculum verified (date and method)
    Dataset counts Measured on the cluster, not estimated measured: true (249 dataset nodes)
    Imaging and pathology results Training and evaluation verified before listing verified on result records; withheld otherwise
    Mirrored external graphs Fingerprint, upstream slices, independent re-extraction, store round trip PROOF.json; see RDF vocabulary

    The markdown atlas¶

    The atlas in atlas/ is the git-backed fact graph where verifiable facts are written as typed markdown pages. Page types (atlas/CLAUDE.md):

    Layer Page types Pages at HEAD
    Human-readable concept-, document- 51 concepts, 1 document
    Machine-readable fact-, entity- 3 facts, 4 entities
    Evaluation dataset-, item-, model-, result- 1 dataset, 2 items, 0 models, 0 results
    Registry source- 7 sources
    Governance dispute-, curator-, domain-, jurisdiction-, strata- 2 domains, 1 jurisdiction, 2 strata
    Navigation map- (maps of content) 1

    Rules the linter enforces (atlas/tools/lint_panatlas.py; CI blocks on error):

    1. Every page has frontmatter with type, slug (equal to the file name), status (draft, in_review, verified, deprecated) and oneliner.
    2. Every wikilink in a frontmatter edge field resolves to an existing page.
    3. prerequisites between concepts stay acyclic.
    4. Every verified fact cites at least one registered source- page (the publish gate).
    5. Every factual bullet carries a provenance tag: src:<slug>@<ver>, conv:YYYY-MM-DD, web:<url>, wikidata:Q<id> or [[document-…]], optionally followed by (confidence: …).
    6. Held-out item answers never live in the public file.

    A fact page is a typed triple: fact_type, subject, predicate, object, polarity, confidence, with about, elaborates, stated_in and cites_source edges. The predicate must be legal for the fact type, from atlas/predicates.yaml:

    fact_type Predicates
    mechanism inhibits, activates, agonist_of, antagonist_of, causes, mediated_by
    association associated_with, risk_factor_for, marker_of
    dosage dose_is, route_is, max_dose_is
    contraindication contraindicated_with, avoid_in
    indication first_line_for, second_line_for, indicated_for
    diagnostic localizes_to, confirmed_by, elevated_in, seen_on
    temporal precedes, rises_after, peaks_at
    epidemiologic prevalence_in, incidence_in
    definitional is_a, part_of, subtype_of

    Edges live on the owning end only: a fact owns its elaborates, about and stated_in edges, and concept, entity and document pages receive them through generated reverse-index blocks that are never hand-edited.

    build_layered.py reads the atlas concept pages (atlas/20-concepts/*.md) to build the concept layer of the served graph: the page's concept_kind sets the node kind (map becomes specialty, the Medicine page becomes root, a gate page becomes gate), and its prerequisites become prereq edges. The verified MeSH mapping then supplies the canonical identity and the hierarchy. The 51 concept pages at HEAD are the medical curriculum, so root is the root of that curriculum, not of the atlas: the atlas itself sits under atlas-root, with one domain node per top-level field (Graph schema).
